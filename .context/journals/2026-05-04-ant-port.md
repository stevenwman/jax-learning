**3/3 seeds PASS Gate 1 + Gate 2.** Acceptance "≥2 of 3 pass" is met with margin.

**Seed 1 is the headline — two different skills tell two different stories:**
- z6=**+199.6 task return** (highest of all 24 z-evals across 3 Ant Classic seeds), stays near origin in the figure (pink trail in central cluster). High return because Ant's survive_reward dominates when the policy stays healthy + minimizes ctrl/contact costs; this is a *stand-upright/shuffle* skill, NOT locomotion.
- z7=-227 task return (negative), gray trail in figure extending ~1.4m in the -x direction. Negative because forward_reward has +x bias, so traveling backward incurs penalty. **This is the trail that drives max-pairwise 1.197m — geometric diversity, not return-positive.**

**Lesson: in Ant, "highest task return" and "biggest xy displacement" can be opposite skills.** Verified by inspecting `projects/skill-discovery/figures/ant_classic_diayn_seed1.png`: z6 (pink, idx 6 of tab10) is in the central cluster; z7 (gray, idx 7) is the long trail. Initial framing of "z6=+199 visible as long trail" was a misread that conflated return rank with figure-spread.

(CheetahRun seeds 0-2 also had positive-return skills, e.g. seed 2 z7=48.2, but Cheetah's task reward is dominated by forward velocity with tiny penalties; positive there ≈ running forward. Not directly comparable to Ant where positive can mean standing still.)

The visual-gate (max-pairwise xy + heading-std) measures geometric diversity, divorced from task return. That's still the canonical DIAYN-paper-style legibility check. Even when no skill walks forward productively, the radial fan-out across 8 directions is the recognizable artifact.

Classic 27d obs is favorable because it forces the discriminator to use motion not contact-force minutiae for differentiation. v5 (105d, with cfrc) lets the discriminator hide skill diffs in contact-force minutiae → tighter xy spread despite higher heading variety (visible in `projects/skill-discovery/figures/ant_classic_vs_v5_seed0.png`).
