## MEM_FRACTION: territory vs. fragmentation

User pushback: "what's the point of picking conservative when we don't even know how much it will take? what if I wanna test with double the network size or num env?" Valid — hardcoding 0.55 just moves the OOM boundary without fixing anything, and constrains future experiments.

The actual mechanism: `XLA_PYTHON_CLIENT_PREALLOCATE=true` (JAX default) grabs a slab at startup; BFC coalescing inside that slab prevents fragmentation. `MEM_FRACTION` only controls slab size. Changing it doesn't improve fragmentation — it only changes where OOM manifests (JAX-side vs Warp-side).

Decision: remove all `setdefault` calls from train scripts. Users tune via shell env when they hit OOM. Full strategy documented in [`lessons/memory_tuning.md`](../lessons/memory_tuning.md) — signals, workflow, and contested-GPU patterns.

## Torque-speed actuator: motivation + implementation

User asked which frameworks do "real motor models instead of just torque actuators." Web-fetched MJLab's `actuator/` source: three models exist (`IdealPdActuator`, `DcActuator`, `LearnedMlpActuator`). The "DC motor" is a misnomer — not an electromechanical sim, just a linear torque-speed curve `τ_limit = τ_stall · max(1 - |q̇|/q̇_max, 0)`. Learned MLP requires real robot data; torque-speed curve is drop-in.

Verified motor constants from two Unitree repos (both authoritative):
- `unitree_mujoco/unitree_robots/go2/go2.xml` → MJCF `actuator_ctrlrange` = 23.7 Nm (hip/thigh), 45.43 Nm (calf)
- `unitree_rl_gym/resources/robots/go2/urdf/go2.urdf` → URDF velocity limits = 30.1 rad/s (hip/thigh), 20.07 rad/s (calf)

URDF lists calf stall as 35.55 Nm vs MJCF's 45.43. Moot for our implementation since we read stall from MJCF at runtime.

Built:
- New constant `MOTOR_VELOCITY_LIMIT_PER_JOINT_TYPE = (30.1, 30.1, 20.07)` in [`go2_constants.py`](../../jax_rl/envs/locomotion/go2_constants.py).
- [`Go2WarpEnv.__init__`](../../jax_rl/envs/locomotion/go2_warp_base.py) builds per-joint `_stall_torque` (from MJCF, remapped via `argsort(act_to_joint)` to joint order) and `_velocity_limit` (tiled URDF values). Flag `_torque_speed_model` read from `config.torque_speed_model`.
- Shared helper `_apply_torque_speed_limit(tau_joint, dq)` on the base. Called from each env's `substep` after PD. Zero overhead when flag off (Python bool → branch baked at trace time).
- `torque_speed_model=False` added to `default_config()` for joystick + bongo.
- New registered env `Go2WarpJoystickFlatTorqueSpeed` in [`env_setup.py`](../../jax_rl/training/env_setup.py) with dedicated `_warp_default_config_torque_speed()` factory. Had to use a factory instead of `functools.partial(..., config_overrides=...)` because `registry.load()` passes `config_overrides=None` which clobbers the partial's setting.
- Presets for `Go2WarpJoystickFlatTorqueSpeed` across PPO, FastSAC, FlashSAC (same hyperparams as base variant).
- 12 unit tests in `TestTorqueSpeedModel` covering: shape + values of stall/velocity arrays, helper no-op when disabled, clip at zero/half/full/beyond velocity, symmetric clip, small-torque passthrough, end-to-end step no-NaN. All pass.

## Trajectory analysis — why the videos look identical

Recorded 3 rollouts (baseline pol in baseline env, ts pol in ts env, baseline pol in ts env as cross-test). Visually indistinguishable. Loaded `qvel` from each `_traj.npz` and checked how often the clip fires:

| | baseline→baseline | baseline→ts | ts→ts |
|---|---|---|---|
| Peak \|q̇\| (rad/s) | 15.6 | 15.6 | 13.7 |
| % steps/joints > 50% vlim | 0.38% | 0.38% | 0.45% |
| % saturated (>100% vlim) | **0%** | **0%** | **0%** |
| Mean torque-speed scale | 0.918 | 0.918 | 0.920 |

Peak joint velocity ~15 rad/s, velocity limits 20–30 rad/s. Closest approach: calf at 62% of limit. **Zero saturations across 1000 steps × 12 joints.**

`baseline→baseline` and `baseline→ts` trajectories match to 4 floating-point digits — same policy, dormant clip, same dynamics. Makes sense: the clip only fires at high joint velocities, which don't occur during 1 m/s flat-terrain walking.

`ts→ts` has slightly lower peak velocities (13.7 vs 15.6). The torque-speed-trained policy learned a marginally more conservative gait, preemptively staying away from the limit even though it's rarely active. Small shaping effect.

## When torque-speed will actually matter

Not during baseline joystick locomotion. Will activate under:
- Commanded sprint (>2 m/s) — joint velocity scales with gait frequency.
- Jumps and recovery motions.
- Push-force disturbances — transient spikes.
- Aggressive angular velocity commands.

Conclusion: torque-speed is ~free insurance. Adopt as default when introducing push curriculum or sprint commands. Not worth forcing everywhere else — no eval gain, and the baseline `Go2WarpJoystickFlat` env remains reproducible for all historical benchmarks.
